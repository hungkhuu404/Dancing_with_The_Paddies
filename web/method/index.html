Data used

NISAR L2 GCOV radar observations provide the HH and HV record. ESA WorldCover 2021 supplies land-cover context. OpenStreetMap supplies geographic context and place names. These sources have different dates and purposes; land cover is not a current ground survey.

From radar to a water calendar

Radar pixels are summarised into the published block grid. The processing pipeline applies the recorded HH and HV thresholds and exports water (W), no water (N), or no image (−) for each acquisition. The interface reads those exported states; it does not classify the images again.

Per-acquisition values below take precedence over the default. Both channels must satisfy their thresholds. Values are displayed to three decimal places; the metadata retains their original precision.

Three answers for each block

Arrival and recession are bounded by satellite visits. Duration is reported as a range. A first or last water observation does not establish the exact beginning or end of flooding. A single water observation is not yet confirmed.

The map's selected-date view can carry the last valid observation through a gap; carried states are faded and labelled. Calendar columns never do this.

How the hamlet table is calculated

Blocks are grouped by their nearest named hamlet. Each percentage is W ÷ (W + N) for that acquisition, using the filtered series. All grid blocks are the same size, so this is also the water share of observed block area. The percentage is withheld when fewer than half of a group's blocks were observed. Unknown states are not counted as dry, and earlier observations are not carried into the table. Colours use five 20-percentage-point bands. Rows are ordered by their first observed W; this is not a ranking of exact flood-onset dates.

Rows group blocks by nearest hamlet. They are not official boundaries.

Limits

Visits leave gaps
Each track revisits about every 12 days. Combining tracks adds observations, but water can arrive or recede between visits.
Images arrive later
Image availability can lag acquisition by about two weeks. This is not a live flood alert. Publication delay is not measured by this app.
No official subregion boundaries
Nearest-hamlet groups are an organising aid. They do not identify irrigation compartments, gate ownership or administrative boundaries.
No methane measurement
The record describes water observations. An IPCC factor, if applicable, is context for an estimate, not a methane measurement.

Radar does not measure water depth here. Dry, flat surfaces can resemble water and date arrival too early; vegetation and surface conditions can also affect the return. Missing images remain uncertain.

Format and cross-file consistency checks do not measure classification accuracy. Agreement scores from reference comparisons are not displayed here.

AI assistance and illustration

Language-model assistance was used for web code and interface copy. Published water states, dates, counts and thresholds come from the data team's files. A language model does not fill missing observations or generate the water record.

The landscape, vegetation, buildings, traffic, gate animations and radar sweep are illustrative. They are not surveyed field geometry, measured depth, reconstructed gate operations or actual radar brightness. The radar view uses its labelled reference image. The regional overview is geographic illustration, not elevation. Ambient audio is procedurally synthesised illustration, not a field recording. Tour narration uses the Jon voice synthesised with ElevenLabs and served as static audio. This disclosure covers the web implementation; other submission materials require their own disclosure.
